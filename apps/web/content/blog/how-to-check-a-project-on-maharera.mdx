## Why check MahaRERA before paying a booking amount?

Because the MahaRERA project page is the promoter's own legal disclosure, and it is the version the regulator holds the promoter to. A brochure or site-office presentation can say anything; the registered completion date, approved plans and progress certificates on MahaRERA are what the promoter is answerable for. MahaRERA itself has urged buyers to verify key project details on its website before making any financial commitment.

Do this check before you pay anything beyond a small, refundable amount. Under Section 13 of the RERA Act, a promoter cannot take more than 10% of the flat's cost until an agreement for sale is executed and registered, so the booking stage is when your money is most exposed and your information is thinnest.

## How do you find the project on MahaRERA?

Start from the registration number, not the project name. MahaRERA requires every advertisement for a registered project to show its registration number and a QR code that opens the registration details, along with the regulator's website address; an April 2025 circular requires these to be displayed prominently. The rule applies to newspaper ads, hoardings, social media and WhatsApp messages alike.

1. Note the registration number from the ad or ask the sales team for it in writing.
2. Scan the QR code, or go to the MahaRERA website and use the registered projects search.
3. Check that the promoter's name, project name, address and phase or wing match what you are being sold. Large projects are often registered in phases, each with its own number and completion date.
4. If no registration number is offered for an under-construction project, stop there.

## What should you read on the project page?

The project page and its uploaded documents carry more than most buyers look at. These are the parts that matter for a booking decision.

| What to check | What you are looking for |
|---|---|
| Proposed and revised completion date | The date the promoter has committed to MahaRERA. Compare it with the possession date in the sales pitch. |
| Building plan and layout approval | Plans approved by the local planning authority (PMC, PCMC or PMRDA, depending on location). |
| Commencement certificate | It states the floors up to which construction is permitted. If you are offered a flat above that floor, ask why. |
| Legal title report and encumbrances | Who owns the land and whether it is mortgaged or charged. |
| Proforma allotment letter and agreement for sale | These should follow MahaRERA's model agreement; any deviation must be highlighted in a different colour. |
| Apartment details | Carpet area, number of flats, floors and covered parking as registered. |
| Bookings | How many flats are shown as booked. |

Read the draft agreement before you sign the allotment letter. It should specify the flat number, carpet area, parking, amenities and the completion date, and the price should be stated on a carpet-area basis.

## How do you read the quarterly progress reports?

Look at the latest quarterly progress report (QPR) and compare it with the one a year earlier. Promoters must upload QPRs every three months, including an architect's certificate on work completed (Form 1), an engineer's certificate (Form 2) and a chartered accountant's certificate on money received and spent (Form 3), along with booking numbers and any changes to the approved plans.

What the QPRs tell you:

- **Pace.** If the percentage of work completed has barely moved over several quarters, the completion date on the page is unlikely to hold.
- **Money.** The CA certificate shows whether spending on the project is keeping up with collections. Under RERA, 70% of booking money must go into a dedicated project account and can be withdrawn only against these certificates.
- **Discipline.** Missing reports are a warning in themselves. After the April 2026 deadline for the January–March quarter, MahaRERA issued show-cause notices to 8,212 projects statewide for not updating QPRs, and Pune district had the highest number, at 1,957. The regulator warned of possible cancellation of registration, frozen bank accounts and a halt on sale registrations.

## How do you check complaints, litigation and the promoter's record?

Check three things: complaints against this project, litigation listed on the project page, and what the promoter has delivered before.

- **Litigation.** The project page lists litigation, if any, and its status. A case over the land title is far more serious than a routine dispute with a contractor; ask a lawyer which kind it is.
- **Complaints.** MahaRERA publishes complaints and non-compliance applications against projects and promoters. Read the orders, not just the count. A few complaints on a large project may be normal; repeated orders for delayed possession or refunds are not.
- **Promoter history.** The promoter's disclosures include projects it launched in the past five years, completed or ongoing. Search those projects on MahaRERA too. Were they completed close to their registered dates? Do any appear as lapsed?
- **Suspended and lapsed lists.** MahaRERA maintains lists of non-compliant projects and has asked buyers to consult them before deciding. In December 2024 it issued notices to 10,773 lapsed projects across the state, 3,406 of them in the Pune region, and in January 2025 it suspended registrations and froze bank accounts of about 1,950 projects that had not filed completion documents or extension requests.

## How do you verify the agent's MahaRERA number?

Ask the agent or firm for its MahaRERA agent registration number and search it in the agent section of the MahaRERA website. MahaRERA has asked buyers to deal only with registered agents.

- Confirm the registration is current, not expired or suspended.
- Since 1 January 2024, MahaRERA requires agents to hold a certificate of competency, earned by training and passing an exam. In May 2024 it suspended about 20,000 agent registrations for not having one.
- For a firm, ask the person handling your purchase for the firm's registration and their own certificate of competency.

## What do red flags look like?

Any one of these is a reason to pause and ask questions; two or more is a reason to walk away until they are answered.

- [ ] No registration number in the ad, or the number belongs to a different phase or wing
- [ ] Possession date promised in sales talks is earlier than the date on MahaRERA
- [ ] Completion date already extended, with little progress shown in recent QPRs
- [ ] QPRs missing for recent quarters
- [ ] Project shown as lapsed, suspended or under abeyance
- [ ] Flat offered above the floors allowed by the commencement certificate
- [ ] Title report shows a pending land dispute or an unexplained mortgage
- [ ] Draft agreement differs from the model agreement without highlighted deviations
- [ ] Pressure to pay more than 10% before the agreement is registered
- [ ] Agent cannot show a valid MahaRERA registration
