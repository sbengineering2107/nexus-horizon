## What rental yield do Pune flats earn?

**As of late 2026, gross rental yield on Pune flats is broadly 3% to 5% a year.** Estimates differ by source and method, so it helps to see them side by side.

| Source | What it measures | Pune figure |
|---|---|---|
| Global Property Guide (2026 page, data date not stated) | Average gross yield across listings | 5.2% |
| NoBroker (December 2025) | Typical residential range | 3% to 5% |
| ANAROCK (Q1 2024) | Average yield in Pune | 3.85%, up from 3.3% in 2019 |

The variation is mostly about unit size. Global Property Guide's figures are 6.14% for 1 BHK, 4.15% for 2 BHK and 4.00% for 3 BHK. NoBroker gives 4% to 6% for 1 BHK and studios, 3.5% to 5% for 2 BHK and 2.8% to 4% for 3 BHK. Smaller flats rent for more per rupee invested.

These are **gross** figures. Global Property Guide estimates that net yields are typically 1.5 to 2 percentage points lower, which puts many Pune flats at roughly 1% to 3.5% net, before income tax.

## How do you calculate rental yield?

**Gross yield = annual rent ÷ purchase price × 100.** Net yield subtracts the costs of owning and letting the flat before dividing.

Use the full cost of the flat as the denominator for a truer picture: the price plus stamp duty, registration and any interiors you add to get it rented.

### Worked example

This is an illustration with assumed figures, not a quote for any project. Say you buy a 2 BHK for ₹80 lakh and let it at ₹26,000 a month.

**Gross yield:** ₹26,000 × 12 = ₹3,12,000 ÷ ₹80,00,000 = **3.9%**

Now the costs, again assumed for illustration:

| Cost | Assumption | Per year |
|---|---|---|
| Society maintenance | ₹4,000 a month, paid by owner | ₹48,000 |
| Property tax | Full rate, no owner-occupier concession | ₹15,000 |
| Vacancy | One month between tenants | ₹26,000 |
| Brokerage | One month's rent every two years | ₹13,000 |
| Repairs and repainting | Allowance | ₹15,000 |
| **Total** | | **₹1,17,000** |

**Net yield before tax:** (₹3,12,000 − ₹1,17,000) ÷ ₹80,00,000 = ₹1,95,000 ÷ ₹80,00,000 = **2.44%**

**After income tax:** rent actually received is ₹2,86,000. Less property tax of ₹15,000 gives ₹2,71,000; less the 30% standard deduction (₹81,300) leaves ₹1,89,700 taxable. At an assumed 30% slab, tax is about ₹56,910, before cess. That takes the net to about ₹1,38,090, or **roughly 1.7%** on the price.

You can run your own numbers in the [rental yield calculator](/calculators/rental-yield-calculator), which accounts for maintenance, property tax and vacancy.

## Which costs reduce net yield?

**Five costs and tax do most of the damage.** Check each one before you trust a yield figure.

1. **Society maintenance.** Who pays it is a term of the rent agreement. If you pay, it comes straight off the rent.
2. **Property tax.** Pune Municipal Corporation's 40% property tax concession is for owner-occupied homes only. In December 2024 it withdrew the concession for over 2.2 lakh properties found to be rented out or housing multiple families. A let-out flat pays the full rate.
3. **Vacancy.** Even one empty month a year removes about 8% of the gross rent.
4. **Brokerage.** In Pune it is typically one month's rent, and it can be charged each time you find a new tenant.
5. **Repairs and upkeep.** Painting, plumbing and appliance replacement between tenants.
6. **Income tax.** Rent is taxed as income from house property. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, in force from 1 April 2026, municipal taxes paid are deducted first, then a flat 30% standard deduction is allowed. Interest on a loan taken to buy the flat is also deductible. Actual repairs and maintenance are not deducted separately; the 30% is meant to cover them.

There are also one-off costs at the start of each tenancy. In Maharashtra, every leave-and-licence agreement must be registered, with stamp duty at 0.25% of the rent and deposit-linked amount plus a ₹1,000 registration fee in municipal areas. The agreement usually says who pays.

## How do location and property type change yield?

**Yield is highest where rents are driven by steady job demand and prices are still moderate.** In a November 2025 report, PropertyWala put gross yields at about 3.8% to 4.2% in Hinjewadi Phase 3, 4.0% to 4.3% in Tathawade, 3.7% to 3.9% in Wakad and 3.5% to 4.0% in Kharadi, but about 2.5% in Koregaon Park and about 2% in Baner. Premium localities tend to have high prices that rents do not keep pace with.

ANAROCK's data for Hinjewadi gives a cross-check. At the end of 2024, a 1,000 sq ft 2 BHK there averaged ₹28,000 a month in rent, against a capital value of about ₹7,800 per sq ft. That works out to about 4.3% gross.

For property type:

- **1 BHK and studios** usually give the highest yield, but tenants change more often.
- **2 BHK** is the broadest rental market, with yields close to the city average.
- **3 BHK and larger** usually give lower yields because prices rise faster than rents.
- **Ready-to-move flats** earn rent from day one. An under-construction flat earns nothing until possession, which lowers the effective return over your holding period.

## Should you buy for rental yield or for appreciation?

**At 3% to 5% gross, a Pune flat is rarely bought for rent alone.** Once costs and tax take net yield down to around 1.5% to 3%, most of an investor's return depends on how much the property's value rises over the holding period.

The two do not always move together. ANAROCK's data for 2021 to 2024 shows rents rising faster than prices in some Pune markets:

| Micro-market | Rent growth 2021–24 | Capital value growth 2021–24 |
|---|---|---|
| Hinjewadi | 57% | 37% |
| Wagholi | 65% | 37% |

Rising rents improve yield on the price you paid, but past growth says nothing certain about the future, and no yield or appreciation figure should be treated as a promise. A practical approach is to check that the rent covers your running costs and a reasonable share of any loan EMI, so you are not forced to sell in a weak market.
