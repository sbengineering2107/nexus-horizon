## How much more than the quoted price will I actually pay?

Expect to pay roughly 7–12% more than the agreement value in government charges alone, and more once loan, society and fit-out costs are added. The biggest items are stamp duty (7% in Pune municipal limits for most buyers) and, for under-construction homes, GST at 5%. Everything else is smaller individually but adds up to several lakh.

The quoted "all-inclusive" price on a brochure usually means the agreement value, sometimes with parking and a few charges folded in. Ask for a written cost sheet that lists each item separately before you pay a booking amount.

## What are stamp duty and registration charges in Pune?

Within Pune municipal limits, stamp duty is 7% for a man buying alone or for a joint purchase that includes a man. This is made up of 5% stamp duty, 1% metro cess and 1% local body tax. A woman buying alone, or with only women co-owners, pays 6% because of Maharashtra's 1% concession for women on residential property. The earlier condition that she could not sell to a male buyer within 15 years was removed in 2023.

Registration fee is 1% of the value, capped at ₹30,000. Any flat above ₹30 lakh therefore pays the flat ₹30,000.

### Where does the ready reckoner come in?

Stamp duty and registration are calculated on the higher of your agreement value and the government's ready reckoner (RR) value for that location and property type. If you negotiate a price below the RR value, you still pay duty on the RR figure. Maharashtra kept RR rates for 2026–27 unchanged from 2025–26, effective 1 April 2026.

You can try different values and buyer types on our [stamp duty calculator](/calculators/stamp-duty-calculator).

## Do I pay GST on a flat in Pune?

Only on under-construction flats bought from a developer:

| Type of flat | GST on price |
|---|---|
| Under-construction, affordable (up to ₹45 lakh, carpet area up to 60 sq m in metro cities or 90 sq m elsewhere; Pune is not a metro for this rule) | 1%, without input tax credit |
| Under-construction, all other residential | 5%, without input tax credit |
| Ready-to-move with completion or occupancy certificate | None |
| Resale flat | None |

This is the single biggest difference between buying under construction and buying ready. On a ₹1 crore flat, it is ₹5 lakh. If you are weighing the two, our page on [ready-to-move homes](/services/ready-to-move) explains the other trade-offs, such as possession timing and rent paid meanwhile.

## What other charges does the developer collect?

### Parking

Under the RERA definition, open parking is a common area and cannot be sold as a separate unit. Covered parking spaces and garages can be sold. In practice, check whether parking is included in the agreement value or priced separately, and make sure the allotted space is identified in writing. Ask for the parking type and number to be written into the allotment letter and agreement.

### Maintenance deposit and advance maintenance

Most developers collect maintenance in advance at possession, often for a year, plus in some projects a separate corpus or sinking-fund deposit. Amounts vary widely by project size and amenities, so ask for the exact months and rate per square foot in the cost sheet. Once the society is running, note that GST at 18% applies if your monthly contribution exceeds ₹7,500.

### Society formation and legal charges

Expect line items for society formation and registration, share money, and the developer's legal or documentation charges. These are usually fixed sums collected around possession. They are modest compared with stamp duty, but they should be listed and not added later.

### Floor rise and preferred location

Higher floors and garden-, pool- or corner-facing units often carry extra charges. Since these increase the agreement value, they also increase stamp duty and GST.

## What does the home loan add?

- **Processing fee:** SBI's card rate for a regular home loan is 0.35% of the loan, with a minimum of ₹5,000 and a maximum of ₹15,000 for salaried borrowers (₹18,000 for non-salaried), plus GST. Other lenders commonly charge 0.5–1%, so on a ₹75 lakh loan this can range from ₹15,000 to about ₹75,000 plus GST.
- **Legal and valuation fees:** Lenders charge for title search and property valuation. Ask for these separately, as they are not always included in the processing fee.
- **Your own contribution:** RBI caps loans above ₹75 lakh at 75% of the property value. Stamp duty and registration are excluded from the property cost for this calculation, so you pay them in full from your own funds.

## Is brokerage payable?

It depends on the deal. For resale flats, brokerage is commonly 1–2% of the value. MahaRERA's model agreement now includes a clause where the fee payable to a registered agent, and who pays it, is written into the agreement for sale. Whoever you deal with, confirm they are MahaRERA-registered and that the fee is agreed in writing before site visits turn into negotiations.

## Worked example: a ₹1 crore flat in Pune

Assumptions: flat within Pune municipal limits; agreement value ₹1 crore and higher than the ready reckoner value; buyer is a man or a joint buyer including a man; home loan of ₹75 lakh; non-affordable category.

| Item | Under-construction | Ready-to-move (with OC) |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement value | ₹1,00,00,000 | ₹1,00,00,000 |
| GST at 5% | ₹5,00,000 | Nil |
| Stamp duty at 7% | ₹7,00,000 | ₹7,00,000 |
| Registration fee (capped) | ₹30,000 | ₹30,000 |
| Loan processing fee | ₹15,000 to ₹75,000 + GST | ₹15,000 to ₹75,000 + GST |
| **Total before variable costs** | **about ₹1.12–1.13 crore** | **about ₹1.07–1.08 crore** |

Add to this, as quoted by the developer, seller or lender:

1. Parking, if priced separately
2. Advance maintenance and any corpus or sinking-fund deposit
3. Society formation, share money and legal charges
4. Lender's legal and valuation fees
5. Brokerage, if applicable (₹1–2 lakh at 1–2% on a resale deal)
6. Interiors, appliances and moving

With a ₹75 lakh loan, your own money for the under-construction flat starts at about ₹37 lakh (₹25 lakh margin, ₹5 lakh GST and ₹7.3 lakh stamp duty and registration), unless your lender counts GST in the property cost. For a woman buying alone, stamp duty falls to ₹6 lakh, saving ₹1 lakh.

## Checklist before you pay the booking amount

- Written cost sheet listing every charge, with GST shown separately
- Ready reckoner value checked for the exact location
- Parking: included or extra, open or covered, and identified
- Months of advance maintenance and any deposit stated
- Brokerage, if any, agreed in writing
- Lender's processing, legal and valuation fees confirmed
