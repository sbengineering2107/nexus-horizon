## How do the three options compare at a glance?

An under-construction flat often has a lower price and a staggered payment plan, but you pay GST and carry delay risk. A ready-to-move flat costs more per square foot but has no GST and no wait. A resale flat also has no GST, and you see exactly what you are buying, but the paperwork and loan checks fall on you.

| | Under-construction (new project) | Ready-to-move (new, with OC) | Resale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price and negotiation | Often lower, especially at launch | Usually higher; room depends on unsold stock | Direct with seller; depends on their urgency |
| GST | 5% (1% for affordable), without input tax credit | None if the full price is paid after completion or first occupation | None |
| Stamp duty and registration | 7% in Pune city limits (6% for a woman alone) + registration up to ₹30,000 | Same | Same |
| TDS by buyer | 1% at ₹50 lakh or more | Same | Same |
| Possession | Future date, with delay risk | Immediate | After registration and handover by the seller |
| RERA protection | Full: escrow, 10% advance cap, delay interest | Defect liability still runs | Limited; the building may be past the defect period |
| Payment | Construction-linked instalments | Lump sum, mostly loan-funded | Lump sum, mostly loan-funded |
| What you can inspect | Sample flat and plans | The actual flat | The actual flat and how the society runs |
| Main checks | Project's MahaRERA page and progress | OC, carpet area, handover condition | Title chain, society NOC and dues, building age |

For a line-by-line cost breakdown, see the [total cost of buying a flat in Pune](/blog/total-cost-of-buying-a-flat-in-pune).

## How much does GST change the price?

Under-construction residential flats carry 5% GST without input tax credit, or 1% if the flat qualifies as affordable (up to ₹45 lakh and up to 90 sq m carpet area in a non-metro city such as Pune under the GST definition). On a ₹1 crore flat, 5% is ₹5 lakh.

These rates do not apply where the entire consideration is received after the completion certificate or first occupation, whichever is earlier. So a ready-to-move flat bought outright after OC, and any resale flat, carries no GST. If you booked a flat before completion and the OC arrives while you are still paying, the instalments you pay remain under the GST rules, so ask the developer how they are billing.

## Are stamp duty and TDS the same for all three?

Yes for stamp duty and registration. Both are charged on the higher of agreement value and ready reckoner value, whoever the seller is. You can check the figure on our [stamp duty calculator](/calculators/stamp-duty-calculator).

TDS also applies to all three, not only to resale. When you buy property worth ₹50 lakh or more from a resident seller, you must deduct 1% from the payment and deposit it with the income tax department; for payments from 1 April 2026 this is done through Form 141, which replaced Form 26QB. If the resale seller is a non-resident, different withholding rules apply, so take advice before paying.

## What protects you if an under-construction project is delayed?

The RERA Act, 2016 gives buyers of new projects several protections:

1. **10% advance cap (Section 13):** the promoter cannot take more than 10% of the flat's cost as an advance or application fee before a registered agreement for sale.
2. **70% escrow (Section 4(2)(l)(D)):** 70% of the money collected from buyers must go into a separate bank account, used only for that project's construction and land cost, with withdrawals certified against progress.
3. **Delay remedies (Section 18):** if possession is not given by the agreed date, you can withdraw and claim a refund with interest and compensation, or stay and receive interest for every month of delay until possession.
4. **Defect liability (Section 14(3)):** structural or workmanship defects reported within five years of handover must be fixed by the promoter within 30 days.

These reduce risk but do not remove it. Check the project's registration, completion date and progress reports first, as explained in [how to check a project on MahaRERA](/blog/how-to-check-a-project-on-maharera).

## How does paying in stages affect your monthly budget?

Under-construction flats are usually sold on a construction-linked plan, and lenders must release the home loan in stages tied to construction, not upfront, under RBI instructions, and the National Housing Bank reminded housing finance companies of the same rule in November 2025.

The catch is the overlap. During construction you typically pay interest (pre-EMI) on the amount disbursed so far, while still paying rent or an existing EMI. Budget for that overlap over the full construction period plus a cushion for delay. With a ready-to-move or resale flat, the full EMI starts at once but rent stops soon after.

## How do home loans differ for each option?

- **Under-construction:** stage-wise disbursement, after the lender checks the project's approvals.
- **Ready-to-move:** a single disbursement against the registered agreement and OC.
- **Resale:** the lender runs its own legal check on the title chain and a valuation. It lends on its valuation, so if you agree a price above it, the difference comes from your pocket.

Building age matters for resale. There is no fixed age limit, but lenders generally want the building's remaining life, as judged by their engineer, to be at least as long as the loan tenure. An older building can mean a shorter tenure and higher EMI, or a refusal from cautious lenders. If eligibility is tight, see [how to improve home loan eligibility](/blog/how-to-improve-home-loan-eligibility), and test EMIs on our [home loan calculator](/calculators/home-loan-calculator).

## What can you actually inspect before buying?

With an under-construction flat you see only a sample flat, which is often furnished and may not match your floor, view or orientation, so rely on the plan and specifications in the agreement. A ready-to-move or resale flat lets you check light, ventilation, seepage and fittings in your actual unit; a resale also shows how the building has aged.

## How is carpet area measured?

Under Section 2(k) of the RERA Act, carpet area is the net usable floor area inside the flat, including internal partition walls but excluding external walls, service shafts, exclusive balconies or verandahs and exclusive open terraces. New-project agreements must use this definition. Older resale agreements and listings often quote built-up or super built-up area, which is larger, so compare prices per square foot of carpet area only.

## What about amenities, maintenance and the society?

In a new project the developer must enable formation of the society; under Section 11(4)(e), where local law is silent, this is within three months of a majority of flats being booked. Until the society takes over, the developer collects and spends maintenance, and some amenities may still be unfinished. In an established building the society, its maintenance charges and the state of the amenities are visible today, so running costs are easier to judge.

## What extra checks does a resale flat need?

Beyond the title documents in our [resale flat document checklist](/blog/resale-flat-document-checklist-pune), confirm three things:

- **Society NOC and membership:** the society's consent to transfer the share certificate and admit you as a member.
- **Transfer premium:** the Bombay High Court reaffirmed in 2025 that a co-operative housing society cannot collect anything beyond the government-prescribed transfer premium (capped at ₹25,000 in municipal corporation areas such as Pune) when admitting a new member, whatever the charge is called.
- **Dues:** a written statement from the society that maintenance, sinking fund and other charges are paid up to the date of transfer, plus paid property tax and utility bills.

## Which option suits whom?

- **Under-construction** suits buyers who can wait until the RERA completion date and some margin beyond it, can carry rent and pre-EMI together, and want a newer building at a lower entry price.
- **Ready-to-move** suits buyers who need to move soon or want to stop paying rent, and who prefer to avoid GST and delay risk even at a higher price.
- **Resale** suits buyers who want a specific, established location, want to see the society and neighbours first, and are comfortable doing thorough document and loan checks.
