## Why check documents before the token, not after?

Because a token is often the point where the seller stops negotiating and you lose leverage. Once you have paid, a missing document becomes your problem to chase, and getting the token back depends on what the receipt says. Ask for copies of the documents below first, have a property lawyer review them, and write into the token receipt that the payment is refundable if title or documents turn out to be defective.

You do not need originals at this stage. Clear photocopies or scans are enough to spot most problems; you will see the originals before registration.

## What makes up the title chain?

The title chain is every registered transfer of the flat from the first buyer to the current seller, with no gaps. For a resale flat in Pune that usually means:

1. **The original agreement for sale** between the builder and the first buyer.
2. **Every later sale agreement or deed**, one for each change of owner.
3. **Index II for each of those registrations.** Index II is issued by the Sub-Registrar's office and summarises a registered document: the parties, the property description, the date and the registration number. You can search registered documents on the IGR Maharashtra website or get copies from the Sub-Registrar.
4. **Any document that explains a gap**, such as a probate, succession certificate, release deed or gift deed where a flat passed by inheritance or within a family.

Check that names, flat number, wing, floor and area match across every document. A flat that was inherited but never formally transferred, or an owner who has died with heirs not yet on record, needs a lawyer's opinion before any money changes hands.

## Has anything been mortgaged or charged against the flat?

Ask your lawyer for a search of registered documents against the property. The IGR Maharashtra online search is a free first check but not a certified record; a certified encumbrance certificate can be applied for at the Sub-Registrar's office. Your lawyer will advise how many years back to search; older or inherited properties usually need a longer search.

If the seller has a running home loan, the bank holds the originals. In that case you need:

- [ ] The bank's letter stating the outstanding amount, so you can pay the bank directly
- [ ] After closure, the bank's no-dues or loan-closure letter
- [ ] The complete set of original documents released by the bank
- [ ] Removal of any charge the bank registered, where one exists

RBI rules require lenders to return original property documents and remove registered charges within 30 days of full repayment, or pay the borrower ₹5,000 a day for the delay. That helps the seller, but you should still not complete the purchase until the originals are in hand or the release is built into the registration-day payment arrangement.

## Which society documents should you see?

For a flat in a co-operative housing society, the share certificate is as important as the sale agreement. Ask for:

- **Share certificate** in the seller's name, original seen before registration.
- **Society no-dues letter** confirming maintenance, sinking fund and other charges are paid up to date.
- **Society NOC, if the society insists on one.** Under the model bye-laws, an NOC is not on the list of documents needed for transfer, but a society that has not adopted them may still ask for one. A Divisional Joint Registrar quoted on the subject said a society should issue an NOC within a month of an application, and give reasons in writing if it refuses.
- **Transfer charges in writing.** The society's bye-laws cap the transfer premium; get the figure in writing and refuse any "donation" added on top.

If the building has no registered society yet, find out who manages it and whether the land has been conveyed. That is a question for your lawyer, not a deal-breaker by itself.

## Does the building have an occupancy certificate?

It should. The occupancy certificate (OC) is issued by the municipal authority (PMC, PCMC or PMRDA, depending on location) and confirms the building was built as per approved plans and is fit to occupy. Ask for the OC and the commencement certificate, and check the OC on the authority's website using the building name or CTS or survey number where records are online.

Buying in a building without an OC can mean trouble with home loans (banks generally ask for it), society formation, conveyance and utility connections. If part of the building has an OC and part does not, check which part your flat is in.

## Are the property tax and land records in order?

Ask for the latest property tax bill and receipts from PMC or PCMC in the seller's name, and the latest electricity bill, and make clearing any arrears a condition of the sale.

For land records, urban properties in Pune city have a property card from the City Survey Office, identified by a CTS number. The 7/12 extract applies to land still recorded as agricultural or rural, which you may still see in some fringe areas. For a flat, the record relates to the land the building stands on: check that the land described matches the agreement and that there are no adverse entries. A clean, current property card also matters to your lender.

## What are the risks with a power of attorney?

If someone other than the owner is signing for the seller, slow down. The Supreme Court has held that transfers by GPA, or by an agreement plus GPA plus will, do not convey title; immovable property is transferred only by a registered conveyance. In a 2025 case, the court held a sale deed executed by a GPA holder after the original owner's death to be invalid.

If a POA is unavoidable, for example an owner living abroad:

- [ ] The POA is registered and specifically authorises the sale of this flat
- [ ] The owner is alive and confirms the deal directly, ideally by video call and in writing
- [ ] Payment goes to the owner's own bank account, not the attorney's

## What will stamp duty and registration cost on a resale?

The same rates apply to resale as to new flats. In the Pune municipal area:

| Buyer | Stamp duty (incl. 1% metro cess and 1% local body tax) | Registration fee |
|---|---|---|
| Man, or joint buyers | 7% | 1%, capped at ₹30,000 |
| Woman as sole buyer | 6% | 1%, capped at ₹30,000 |

Duty is charged on the higher of the agreement value and the ready reckoner value. For example, on an ₹80 lakh agreement value that is above ready reckoner, a man pays ₹5.6 lakh stamp duty and ₹30,000 registration. Use our [stamp duty calculator](/calculators/stamp-duty-calculator) for your own figures.

If the seller is resident in India and the price is ₹50 lakh or more, you must also deduct 1% TDS from the payment to the seller and deposit it. From 1 April 2026 this is reported in Form 141, which replaced Form 26QB. If the seller is an NRI, different and higher TDS rules apply at any price, so check with a tax adviser before paying.
