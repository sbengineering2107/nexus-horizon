## Leave-and-licence or lease: which one will you sign?

In Pune, most office space is taken on a **leave-and-licence** agreement rather than a lease. The difference is legal, not cosmetic.

| | Leave and licence | Lease |
|---|---|---|
| What you get | Permission to use the premises; no interest in the property | A transfer of the right to enjoy the property, with legal possession |
| Transfer | Personal to you; generally cannot be assigned | Can be assigned and survives a sale of the property |
| End of term | Landlord has a statutory route to recover possession | Governed by the Transfer of Property Act and tenancy law |
| Stamp duty | Worked out on licence fee and deposit | Worked out on market value, depending on term |

Landlords prefer leave and licence because getting the premises back is simpler. For you, the practical points are that the right is personal to your business, and a licence may not bind a new owner if the landlord sells. Ask for a clause that the agreement will be honoured by any buyer. A licence longer than five years is stamped as a lease in Maharashtra, which is one reason most office agreements run for five years or less.

## Is registration compulsory, and what does it cost?

**Yes, every leave-and-licence agreement in Maharashtra must be in writing and registered**, whatever its length. Section 55 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act puts this duty on the landlord, with a penalty of up to three months' imprisonment or a ₹5,000 fine. If an unregistered agreement is disputed, the occupant's version of the terms is presumed correct unless the landlord proves otherwise. The Act does not apply to premises let to banks, public sector undertakings, multinational companies, or private and public limited companies with paid-up share capital of ₹1 crore or more (Section 3), so check which side of that line your business falls on.

Stamp duty is **0.25%** of a figure that adds up:

1. the total licence fee for the whole term,
2. any advance or non-refundable deposit, and
3. a notional interest of 10% a year on the refundable deposit.

Registration costs **₹1,000** in a municipal corporation area and ₹500 in rural areas. The same rates apply to residential and commercial premises for terms up to 60 months.

**Worked example (illustrative):** a three-year licence at ₹1,00,000 a month with a ₹6,00,000 refundable deposit.

- Licence fee for the term: ₹36,00,000
- Notional interest: 10% × ₹6,00,000 × 3 years = ₹1,80,000
- Total: ₹37,80,000 → stamp duty at 0.25% ≈ **₹9,450**, plus ₹1,000 registration

If rent escalates during the term, the higher rent counts in the total. Unless the agreement says otherwise, the occupant usually bears these costs.

## Is the rent charged on carpet or chargeable area?

**Usually on chargeable (super built-up) area, so check how much of it you can actually use.** Chargeable area adds your share of lobbies, lifts, staircases and other common spaces to the carpet area. The gap between the two is the **loading**.

Loading varies by building, and Pune figures are not published consistently. For reference, in the Mumbai Metropolitan Region commercial loading is commonly reported at around 30% to 50%, with some Grade A buildings higher; there is no legal cap. For a fair comparison, convert every quote to a rent per square foot of carpet area.

**Illustration:** 1,500 sq ft chargeable at 40% loading is about 1,071 sq ft of carpet. At ₹80 per sq ft on chargeable area (₹1,20,000 a month), you are really paying about ₹112 per sq ft of usable space.

For context, Knight Frank put Pune's average transacted office rent at ₹83 per sq ft a month in Q3 2026, with vacancy at 13.8%.

## What are the lock-in, escalation and deposit norms?

**There is no fixed rule. These are negotiated, but registered Pune leases show the usual shape.**

### Lock-in

The lock-in is the period during which neither side (or only the landlord) can end the agreement without paying for the remaining months. A registered five-year lease in Kharadi reported in late 2024 had a three-year lock-in. Occupier guides describe two to three years as common, with landlords sometimes asking for longer on large floors.

### Escalation

Two patterns appear in registered Pune leases: **5% a year** (the Kharadi lease above) and **15% every three years** (a large Pune lease reported in March 2025). On a starting rent of ₹1,00,000 a month over five years:

| Structure | Rent in year 5 | Total rent over 5 years |
|---|---|---|
| 5% every year | ₹1,21,551 | about ₹66.3 lakh |
| 15% after year 3 | ₹1,15,000 | about ₹63.6 lakh |

Check whether escalation compounds on the escalated rent, and whether CAM escalates separately.

### Security deposit

Guides commonly cite six to 12 months of rent, refundable without interest. The Kharadi lease carried a ₹8.46 crore deposit against monthly rent of about ₹1.08 crore, roughly eight months. Agree the refund timeline and interest on any delay in writing.

## What does CAM cover?

**Common area maintenance (CAM) is charged on top of rent**, usually per sq ft of chargeable area, and covers housekeeping, security, lift and common-area power, and upkeep. In the Kharadi lease, CAM was ₹13.58 per sq ft a month and rose 5% a year. Ask what CAM includes, on what area it is charged, how it escalates, and whether you can see the accounts. Electricity for your own space, diesel backup and parking are often billed separately.

## Will you get a fit-out or rent-free period?

**Often, if you ask.** A fit-out period is time to set up interiors before rent starts; it is generally rent-free. Reported fit-out periods in India range from about three months to a year depending on the size of the space and the work. Confirm whether CAM and electricity are payable during this time, and from which date the lock-in and escalation are counted.

## How do you exit?

**After the lock-in, exit is normally by written notice, commonly three to six months.** Before signing, settle:

- the notice period for each side, and when it can be served;
- the condition you must return the space in (bare shell or as fitted), and who pays to restore it;
- the deposit refund date, and interest if it is late;
- whether you can transfer the space to a group company.

## Is GST payable on office rent?

**Yes, commercial rent attracts 18% GST.**

- **Landlord registered under GST:** they charge 18% on the invoice. A registered business can usually claim this as input tax credit.
- **Landlord not registered, your business registered:** since 10 October 2024, your business pays the 18% under reverse charge, and can usually claim credit for it.
- **Composition-scheme businesses** were taken out of this reverse charge from 16 January 2025 (Notification 07/2025-CT(Rate)); the period from 10 October 2024 to 15 January 2025 was regularised on an "as is where is" basis.

If your business is not GST-registered, the GST is a real cost, so budget for it.

## What should you check about the building itself?

Before you pay a token, verify:

- [ ] **Occupancy certificate** for the building, and that the unit is approved for commercial or office use, not residential.
- [ ] **Title and authority:** the person signing owns the unit or is authorised, and any mortgage lender has consented.
- [ ] **Fire compliance:** under the Maharashtra Fire Prevention and Life Safety Measures Act, 2006, owners and occupiers must file a Form B certificate from a licensed agency twice a year, in January and July. Ask for the latest one.
- [ ] **Society or association no-objection**, where the building requires it for a commercial occupant.
- [ ] **Power load, parking allotment and signage rights** written into the agreement.
